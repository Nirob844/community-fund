The Live Local Act, explained

What the law does, what it requires, and why residents believe the Prospect Road site does not qualify.

What the law does

Florida's Live Local Act (first passed in 2023 and amended in 2024, 2025 and 2026) requires counties and cities to approve certain apartment projects without a rezoning or a public hearing. For counties, the rules are in section 125.01055(7) of the Florida Statutes.

A project can use the law when, among other things:

  • the land is zoned for commercial, industrial or mixed use;
  • at least 40 percent of the units are rented as affordable (up to 120 percent of area median income) for at least 30 years.

Qualifying projects can be built to the highest density allowed anywhere in the county and to the height of the tallest nearby building, with staff approval instead of a vote by elected commissioners.

Why it matters to taxpayers

Separate Florida law (section 196.1978) gives Live Local projects of more than 70 units a property-tax exemption on qualifying affordable units. Counties may opt out of only part of that exemption. For a 642-unit project, residents estimate the tax the property would no longer pay at roughly $0.9 million to $1.9 million a year, depending on assessed value. That is an estimate, not an official figure.

The Prospect Road site

A 642-unit apartment plan has been filed for about 29 acres between Prospect Road and Tuttle Avenue in unincorporated Manatee County. The land is zoned A-1 (agricultural) with a residential future land use. In 2025 the County Commission denied a smaller, 175-unit plan for the same land after a public hearing.

The county's April 2026 eligibility letter found the site eligible under the Live Local Act. Residents believe that finding is wrong, because:

  • the law requires land zoned for commercial, industrial or mixed use, with the business use allowed by right, without a variance or special approval;
  • on this land, county code allows offices only along an arterial road, and the county's own road table, road map and the state's traffic model all classify the bordering roads as collectors;
  • the applicant itself stated it is not proposing a mixed-use project.

Residents have asked the county, in writing, to correct or withdraw the eligibility finding so that any project on this land goes through the normal public process.

Why this matters beyond our neighborhood

If an eligibility decision can be made on land that does not meet the law's own test, the same thing can happen anywhere in Florida. Getting the facts right protects every community's right to a public hearing.
