The long view

A park for everyone

If the land ever becomes available, the community wants to keep it green, for good.

The idea

The Prospect Road land is about 29 acres of open, agricultural land in a fast-growing part of Manatee County. Instead of 642 apartments, residents imagine a nature park: shade trees kept standing, walking trails, a quiet place for families, and room for rainwater to soak in rather than run into nearby streets and homes.

What a park could include

  • Walking and jogging loop under the existing tree canopy
  • Native-plant meadow and wildlife habitat
  • Stormwater ponds that double as a scenic feature and reduce flooding
  • A small playground and picnic shelter
  • Space for community events, school nature walks and gardening

How the money adds up

The fund's scale has three milestones:

  1. $10,000 to retain a land use attorney
  2. $1,700,000, roughly the land's agricultural (A-1) value
  3. $2,000,000 to buy the land, cover closing costs and set up the park's first year

Large land purchases like this are usually completed with partners such as land trusts, county parks programs and state conservation grants. Community money raised first shows those partners that neighbors are committed.

Honest about the limits

A purchase can only happen if the owner is willing to sell at a fair price. If it never becomes possible, money not spent on the fund's stated purposes is returned to contributors in proportion to what each gave, exactly as shown on the refund shares table.
